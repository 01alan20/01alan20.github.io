Five-year business survival

The answer starts with a cohort: enterprises that entered the employer population in the same year, followed across the same stretch of time. Entry into this population can mean becoming or returning as an employer, rather than first legal registration.

The five-year analysis covers enterprises with salaried employees. Survival requires remaining an employer; leaving the cohort does not necessarily mean closing the business. The 2017–2022 period includes the pandemic and is not a forecast for new openings. The full CNPJ register also includes entities without employees, nonprofits, public bodies and registrations outside Brazil.

Survival by business size and industry

Employer-enterprise cohorts reveal associations with survival. They do not isolate the effect of hiring more people or entering a different industry.

A national average combines businesses with different characteristics. Changing the mix can change the average.

Business registrations

CNPJ describes the national stock of registered establishments. A registration marked active is an administrative status, not proof of revenue, employment, or continuous operation.

READ THE MAP CAREFULLY

A higher observed rate does not identify a “better place” to open a business. A fair location comparison would need historical conditions and comparable business mixes, including industry, size and age.

Current status by registration year

These opening-year groups are observed in the current CNPJ snapshot. Their active shares are not survival probabilities: historical exits, registry coverage and changes in registration rules affect what is visible.

Brazil’s register shows the scale and shape of its businesses. A longitudinal cohort tells us about endurance. Keeping those lenses separate makes both more useful.
