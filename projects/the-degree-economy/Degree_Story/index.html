Higher education and the changing world of work

Are Degrees
Keeping Up?

A university degree does not need to train someone for one specific job. But universities do have a responsibility to help students turn what they learn into the skills, experience and evidence employers can recognize.

Degrees should provide disciplinary depth and durable capabilities. Universities then need to help students apply them and make the transition into work without turning every program into vocational training.

01 / The degree is a foundation

A degree should open possibilities, not prepare someone for only one job.

Some degrees lead directly into professions. Others lead across industries. Both can provide disciplinary knowledge and durable capabilities that travel with graduates.

05 / What universities should do

Preserve depth. Strengthen the path into work.

The response should happen in four places.

01

Renew what universities offer

Use evidence about labor-market change alongside academic mission, research strengths, public need, student demand and institutional capability to decide where programs should be created, combined, redesigned or retired.

Not every shift requires a new degree. Sometimes the better response is a concentration, minor, interdisciplinary pathway, new module or redesign of an existing program.

02

Make the foundation visible across the curriculum

Students should develop disciplinary expertise alongside analytical thinking, communication, research, problem solving, collaboration, adaptability and judgment.

Do not bolt these onto a generic employability module. Build and assess them through the academic work students are already doing.

03

Make meaningful applied experience part of the degree

Projects, placements, co-op, clinical work, fieldwork, research, studios and employer-sponsored assignments should give students repeated opportunities to use what they learn.

Not every student needs the same experience. But meaningful applied experience should become normal rather than exceptional.

04

Career preparation should begin before the final semester

Universities can help students explore occupations earlier, meet employers and alumni, build networks and portfolios, identify their capabilities, prepare for recruitment, and translate academic and applied work into evidence employers understand.
